# Writing style: sound human, not like an LLM

Every line of resume and cover-letter copy should read like a real person wrote it: direct, descriptive, a little casual, and appropriate for the person's level. Recruiters and hiring managers spot AI-assisted writing instantly.

## Voice

- Action verb first, then specifics, then impact. "Cut page load time by 40% by deferring third-party scripts."
- Name the actual stack. Never "various technologies": say "React, FastAPI, and MySQL".
- Quantify when the original does: "thousands of users", "three shipped apps", "50%".
- Plain verbs: built, worked, improved, helped, shipped, delivered, maintained, integrated, debugged, owned.
- Commas and "and" for asides and lists, never dashes.
- Confident first person in the cover letter ("I work across...", "I enjoy..."), not boastful, not hedging.
- One idea per bullet. Don't fuse three wins with semicolons.

## Tone: friendly, eager, humble

- Warm and eager, not blunt. Sound like someone a team would want to work with.
- For stretch roles, lean more humble: own where you're still growing, show you'd love the chance to learn, and address gaps openly. Honest, never self-deprecating.
- Show genuine enthusiasm for the company's actual work (one specific line), not generic flattery.

## Banned patterns

- Em-dashes (—) and spaced en-dashes ( – ) used as pauses. Use a comma, parentheses, or two sentences.
- "It's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y", "not only... but also". State the real point directly.
- leverage, seamless, robust, delve, tapestry, realm, "in the world of", "navigate the landscape".
- cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, game-changer, supercharge, unlock, elevate.
- "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," as openers. Just start the next sentence.
- "In today's...", "When it comes to...", "It's worth noting that", "Needless to say", "At the end of the day".
- "a testament to", "speaks volumes", "paints a picture".
- Exclamation marks in the cover letter.

## Soft preferences

- "used" over "utilized". "because" over "due to the fact that". Concrete numbers over "many" or "several". Active voice over passive.
